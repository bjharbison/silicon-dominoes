Plain-language snapshot. Every sentence below is generated word-for-word from the country's data record — nothing is hand-written. Click a card for its story; switch to Advanced (top right) for the full analytic detail.

Two-axis exposure — US × PRC?

Each dot is a country placed by how deep it sits in both stacks at once — right = more American, up = more Chinese. A country can be deep in both: that's hedging, and it lives in the upper right, away from the dashed either/or line. This is the honest view a single left–right number hides (doc 01 §3.2). Dot color = posture. Click a dot for the full record.

Net tilt, sorted (alignment_index — derived)?

One number per country: US exposure minus PRC exposure. Blue = tilted American, red = tilted Chinese. A reading aid only — it flattens the two-axis picture above, so click any bar to see what it summarizes.

Countries × layers — whose kit, slice by slice?

Each cell: which side's equipment dominates that slice of the stack (blue = mostly American kit installed, red = mostly Chinese). A country running US chips over Chinese telecom gear is normal, not a contradiction — layer-level divergence is the expected outcome. Hatched rows = not enough evidence to score. Hover any cell for the exact shares.

Locked in vs. up for grabs?

Right = harder to leave the current main supplier (lock-in). Up = more actively contested right now (competing offers, contracts up for renewal). Upper-left is the battleground: still cheap to switch and being fought over. Lower-right is settled: expensive to leave and nobody's bidding.

Exposure to each stack, side by side?

The three exposure scores as plain bars — American, Chinese, sovereign/own — per country. The bar version of the scatter above: long blue and long red together means the country is deliberately buying from both sides.

Readiness ladder?

Who could actually absorb and run more AI infrastructure — power, skills, capital, institutions — from frontier-ready (T5) to not yet able (T0). Low readiness doesn't mean irrelevant: strategic weight (votes, bases, minerals, cables) is tracked separately.
